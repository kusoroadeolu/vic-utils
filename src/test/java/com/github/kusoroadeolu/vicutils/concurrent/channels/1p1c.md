# Concurrent Channel Benchmarks - 1P/1C

This document analyzes throughput benchmarks for different channel implementations under **minimal contention** (1 producer, 1 consumer).

---

## Benchmark Setup

- **JMH version:** 1.37
- **JVM:** Java 25, HotSpot 64-Bit Server VM (25+37-LTS-3491)
- **Benchmark mode:** Throughput (ops/s)
- **Warmup:** 3 iterations × 5s each
- **Measurement:** 3 iterations × 5s each
- **Forks:** 3
- **Thread configuration:** 1 producer (platform thread) / 1 consumer (virtual thread)
- **Queue configuration:** Non-fair mode for ArrayBlockingQueue and SynchronousQueue

## Performance Analysis Across Configurations

### Throughput Evolution: From Single Thread to High Contention

| Implementation | 1P-1C | 4P-2C | 4P-4C | Pattern |
|----------------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| **SpinRendezvousChannel** | 5.49M | 6.56M | 7.03M | Scales up with threads |
| **SynchronousQueue** | 5.44M | 3.36M | 4.78M | Irregular scaling |
| **RendezvousChannel** | 111.7K | 22.1K | 23.1K | Degrades severely under contention |
| **ArrayBlockingQueue** | 101.9K | 54.5K | 35.4K | Degrades with more threads |
| **UnBufferedChannel** | 73.7K | 15.6K | 15.9K | Degrades severely under contention |

### Key Observations

#### SpinRendezvousChannel
- **Consistent performance** across all configurations (5.5M - 7.0M ops/s)
- Actually *improves* with more threads (up to 4P-4C)
- Minimal variance at 1P-1C (±347K ops/s, ~6% coefficient of variation)
- Single producer/consumer scenario eliminates most spinning overhead
- Pure CAS operations show their efficiency without contention complexity

#### SynchronousQueue
- **Matches SpinRendezvousChannel** performance at 1P-1C (~5.4M ops/s)
- **Massive variance** (±1.55M ops/s, ~29% coefficient of variation) - highest of all configurations
- Min/max spread of 2.6M ops/s suggests instability in measurement
- Dual stack/queue internal structure may have higher baseline overhead
- Performs worse at 4P-2C (3.36M) than at 4P-4C (4.78M), indicating sensitivity to imbalance

#### RendezvousChannel
- **Best performance at 1P-1C** (111.7K ops/s) - 5× improvement over 4P-4C
- Demonstrates that lock overhead is manageable without contention
- **Extremely stable** (±24.8K ops/s, ~13% coefficient of variation)
- Collapses under multi-threaded load (111K → 22K = 80% degradation)
- Triple condition variable design shows its weakness: great for simple cases, terrible under contention

#### ArrayBlockingQueue
- **3× better at 1P-1C** (102K ops/s) vs 4P-4C (35K ops/s)
- Most consistent degradation pattern as threads increase
- **Very stable** at 1P-1C (±12.7K ops/s, ~7% coefficient of variation)
- Buffering overhead less impactful than lock contention
- Non-fair mode helps but can't overcome fundamental lock bottleneck

#### UnBufferedChannel
- **Worst overall performer** but shows interesting scaling
- 73.7K at 1P-1C vs 15.6K at 4P-4C (79% degradation)
- **High variance** (±26.7K ops/s, ~22% coefficient of variation) even without contention
- `signalAll()` strategy creates overhead even in simple scenarios
- Similar degradation pattern to RendezvousChannel but starting from lower baseline

## Scaling Characteristics

### How Each Implementation Scales

**SpinRendezvousChannel: Positive Scaling**
- 1P-1C: 5.49M (baseline)
- 4P-2C: 6.56M (+19%)
- 4P-4C: 7.03M (+28%)

More threads = more throughput. Lock-free design allows true parallel scaling without coordination overhead.

**SynchronousQueue: Non-Linear Scaling**
- 1P-1C: 5.44M (baseline)
- 4P-2C: 3.36M (-38%)
- 4P-4C: 4.78M (-12%)

Performs best with minimal threads or balanced workloads. Imbalance (4P-2C) causes significant degradation.

**RendezvousChannel: Inverse Scaling**
- 1P-1C: 111.7K (baseline)
- 4P-2C: 22.1K (-80%)
- 4P-4C: 23.1K (-79%)

Single lock becomes severe bottleneck. Performance hits floor around 22K ops/s regardless of thread count.

**ArrayBlockingQueue: Predictable Degradation**
- 1P-1C: 101.9K (baseline)
- 4P-2C: 54.5K (-47%)
- 4P-4C: 35.4K (-65%)

Linear degradation with thread count. Buffering helps with imbalance (4P-2C performs better than 4P-4C).

**UnBufferedChannel: Severe Degradation**
- 1P-1C: 73.7K (baseline)
- 4P-2C: 15.6K (-79%)
- 4P-4C: 15.9K (-78%)

Similar pattern to RendezvousChannel but from lower starting point. Hits performance floor around 16K ops/s.

## Variance Analysis Across Configurations

### Coefficient of Variation by Configuration

| Implementation                     | 1P-1C | 4P-2C | 4P-4C | Stability Pattern |
|------------------------------------|-------|-------|-------|-------------------|
| **RendezvousChannel**              | ~13% | ~4% | ~4% | Stabilizes under load |
| **SpinRendezvousChannel**          | ~6% | ~6% | ~20% | Unstable at high contention |
| **ArrayBlockingQueue(1 capacity)** | ~7% | ~6% | ~12% | Moderate throughout |
| **UnBufferedChannel**              | ~22% | ~12% | ~7% | Improves under contention |
| **SynchronousQueue**               | **~29%** | ~4% | ~13% | Highly unstable at 1P-1C |

**Surprising finding:** Lock-based implementations (RendezvousChannel, UnBufferedChannel) become *more stable* with increased contention, while SynchronousQueue is *most unstable* at 1P-1C.

## When Contention Disappears

### Lock-Based vs Lock-Free at 1P-1C

At minimal contention, the performance hierarchy shifts dramatically:

**Million ops/s tier (lock-free):**
- SpinRendezvousChannel: 5.49M
- SynchronousQueue: 5.44M

**Hundred-thousand ops/s tier (lock-based, optimized):**
- RendezvousChannel: 111.7K
- ArrayBlockingQueue: 101.9K

**Sub-hundred-thousand ops/s tier (lock-based, unoptimized):**
- UnBufferedChannel: 73.7K

Even with zero contention, lock-based approaches have **49× overhead** compared to pure spinning. This overhead comes from:
- Lock acquisition/release (even uncontended)
- Condition variable signaling
- Memory barriers and happens-before relationships
- Kernel involvement in park/unpark operations

## Design Implications

### What 1P-1C Reveals

**Spinning is fundamentally faster:**
- At 1P-1C, there's no wasted spinning - producer and consumer alternate perfectly
- SpinRendezvousChannel achieves 5.49M ops/s with simple CAS operations
- This represents the *ceiling* for handoff performance on modern CPUs

**Lock overhead is constant:**
- RendezvousChannel's lock operations cost ~5.38M ops/s vs spinning (5.49M - 0.112M)
- This overhead exists even without any contention
- Three condition variables don't matter at 1P-1C - the lock itself is the bottleneck

**Buffering has fixed cost:**
- ArrayBlockingQueue only 9% slower than RendezvousChannel at 1P-1C
- Buffer management overhead is minimal compared to lock cost
- Suggests ABQ's degradation under load is contention, not algorithmic complexity

**Signal strategy matters:**
- UnBufferedChannel 34% slower than RendezvousChannel at same thread count
- `signalAll()` vs targeted signaling creates measurable overhead
- Gap persists even when there's only one thread to wake up

## Recommendations by Use Case

### Low Contention Scenarios (1-2 threads per side)

**Use SpinRendezvousChannel when:**
- Maximum throughput critical (5M+ ops/s needed)
- CPU resources available for spinning
- Sub-microsecond latency required
- Simple, maintainable lock-free code preferred

**Use SynchronousQueue when:**
- Production-ready stability required
- 5M ops/s sufficient but variance concerning
- JDK integration important
- Willing to accept 29% variance for proven implementation

**Use RendezvousChannel when:**
- CSP semantics required
- 100K ops/s sufficient
- Lower variance preferred (13% vs 29% for SynchronousQueue)
- CPU efficiency more important than peak throughput

### What the Numbers Tell Us

1. **Lock-free isn't just faster - it's 49× faster** even without contention
2. **Variance increases matter more than absolute numbers** for production systems
3. **SynchronousQueue's complexity hurts stability** at simple workloads
4. **Lock-based channels excel at different things:** stability (Rendezvous) vs buffering (ABQ)
5. **Scaling behavior is implementation-specific:** spin scales up, locks scale down
6. **Simple designs often win:** SpinRendezvousChannel outperforms sophisticated SynchronousQueue in predictability
