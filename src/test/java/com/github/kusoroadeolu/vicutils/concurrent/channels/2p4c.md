# Concurrent Channel Benchmarks - 2P/4C

This document analyzes throughput benchmarks for different channel implementations under **consumer-heavy imbalance** (2 producers, 4 consumers).

---

## Benchmark Setup

- **JMH version:** 1.37
- **JVM:** Java 25, HotSpot 64-Bit Server VM (25+37-LTS-3491)
- **Benchmark mode:** Throughput (ops/s)
- **Warmup:** 3 iterations × 5s each
- **Measurement:** 3 iterations × 5s each
- **Forks:** 3
- **Thread configuration:** 2 producers (platform threads) / 4 consumers (virtual threads)
- **Queue configuration:** Non-fair mode for ArrayBlockingQueue and SynchronousQueue

## The Mirror Image: 2P-4C vs 4P-2C

### Throughput Comparison: Producer-Heavy vs Consumer-Heavy

| Implementation | 4P-2C | 2P-4C | Change | Pattern |
|----------------|-------|-------|--------|---------|
| **SpinRendezvousChannel** | 6.56M | 4.66M | -29.0% | Symmetric degradation |
| **SynchronousQueue** | 3.36M | 2.89M | -14.0% | Better with fewer producers |
| **ArrayBlockingQueue** | 54.5K | 42.3K | -22.4% | Worse with more consumers |
| **UnBufferedChannel** | 15.6K | 20.7K | +32.7% | Benefits from fewer producers |
| **RendezvousChannel** | 22.1K | 17.6K | -20.4% | Slightly worse |

### What the Mirror Reveals

**The asymmetry tells us something profound:**

1. **4P-2C (few consumers):** Consumers are the bottleneck
2. **2P-4C (few producers):** Producers are the bottleneck

But implementations respond differently to which side bottlenecks:

**SpinRendezvousChannel: Symmetric Response**
- 4P-2C: -6.7% from baseline (4P-4C)
- 2P-4C: -33.7% from baseline (4P-4C)
- **Producers bottleneck hurts more** than consumer bottleneck
- Spinning consumers waste more cycles waiting than spinning producers
- Suggests producer-side spinning is more efficient than consumer-side

**SynchronousQueue: Producer Sensitivity**
- 4P-2C: -29.7% from baseline
- 2P-4C: -39.6% from baseline
- Both imbalances hurt, but **fewer producers hurts most**
- Dual-mode algorithm may optimize for producer availability
- Park/unpark on consumer side may be more efficient

**ArrayBlockingQueue: Inverse Behavior**
- 4P-2C: +53.8% from baseline (fewer consumers helped!)
- 2P-4C: +19.4% from baseline (fewer producers helps less)
- **Put-side contention matters more** than take-side contention
- With 2 producers vs 4: Less put-side contention, but also less throughput
- With 4 consumers vs 2: More take-side contention drains buffer faster

**UnBufferedChannel: Producer Bottleneck Winner**
- 4P-2C: -2.3% from baseline
- 2P-4C: +30.1% from baseline (**improvement!**)
- Fewer producers = less put-side contention on single lock
- More consumers doesn't hurt much (already at lock ceiling)
- Best relative performance in consumer-heavy scenario

## Performance Analysis by Implementation

### SpinRendezvousChannel: Producer Bottleneck Penalty

**Throughput:** 4.66M ops/s (-33.7% vs 4P-4C, -29.0% vs 4P-2C)

**What this reveals:**
- With only 2 producers, can't keep 4 consumers fed
- **Producers become the limiting resource**
- 4 consumer threads spin waiting for 2 producers to generate work
- More total spinning = lower efficiency = higher variance

**Variance:** ±906K ops/s (~19% CV)
- Similar to 4P-4C (±827K, ~12% CV)
- High absolute variance despite lower throughput
- 2M ops/s min/max spread suggests measurement instability
- Spinning consumers create chaotic timing patterns

### SynchronousQueue: Severe Degradation

**Throughput:** 2.89M ops/s (-39.6% vs 4P-4C, -14.0% vs 4P-2C)

**What this reveals:**
- **Worst configuration** for SynchronousQueue across all tested scenarios
- 1.4M ops/s worse than 4P-2C despite same thread count
- Dual-mode algorithm struggles when consumers heavily outnumber producers
- Internal coordination overhead amplified by producer scarcity

**Variance:** ±1.01M ops/s (~21% CV) - **highest variance of any configuration**
- Fork 3 dropped to 1.9M ops/s (33% below average!)
- Min/max spread: 1.4M ops/s (74% of minimum value)
- Suggests algorithmic instability or pathological behavior
- May be hitting edge cases in dual-mode coordination

### ArrayBlockingQueue: Lost the Imbalance Advantage

**Throughput:** 42.3K ops/s (+19.4% vs 4P-4C, -22.4% vs 4P-2C)

**What this reveals:**
- At 4P-2C: Gained 53.8% (fewer consumers = less take-side contention)
- At 2P-4C: Gained only 19.4% (fewer producers = less put-side contention, but also less production)
- **Put-side contention dominates** performance more than take-side
- Buffer drains faster with more consumers, reducing decoupling benefit

**Variance:** ±19.9K ops/s (~28% CV) - **highest variance of any implementation at 2P-4C**
- Massive min/max spread: 21K to 56.7K (2.7× range!)
- Fork 2 had catastrophic degradation (21K minimum)
- Suggests unstable behavior when consumers outnumber producers
- Buffer starvation may cause lock thrashing

### UnBufferedChannel: The Unlikely Beneficiary

**Throughput:** 20.7K ops/s (+30.1% vs 4P-4C, +32.7% vs 4P-2C)

**What this reveals:**
- **Only lock-based implementation that improved** at 2P-4C
- With 2 producers: Less put-side contention on single lock
- With 4 consumers: Take-side already at saturation, doesn't degrade further
- Producer bottleneck helps by reducing lock contention

**Variance:** ±2.7K ops/s (~8% CV)
- Relatively stable for this implementation
- Lower variance than 4P-4C (±1.1K, ~7% CV) or 4P-2C (±3.1K, ~12% CV)
- Fewer producers = simpler coordination = more predictable timing

### RendezvousChannel: Degradation Despite Producer Reduction

**Throughput:** 17.6K ops/s (-24.0% vs 4P-4C, -20.4% vs 4P-2C)

**What this reveals:**
- Lost ground despite fewer producers creating less contention
- Triple condition variable coordination may struggle with imbalance
- Unlike UnBufferedChannel, doesn't benefit from producer reduction
- Suggests coordination overhead dominates over lock contention

**Variance:** ±4.6K ops/s (~16% CV) - **highest for this implementation across all configs**
- Much worse than 4P-4C (±576, ~2.5% CV) or 4P-2C (±810, ~4% CV)
- 8K ops/s min/max spread (63% of minimum)
- Producer scarcity creates timing unpredictability
- Box allocation timing may become more variable

## Variance Analysis: Consumer-Heavy Instability

### Coefficient of Variation Across All Configurations

| Implementation | 1P-1C | 4P-2C | 4P-4C | 2P-4C | Pattern |
|----------------|-------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| **RendezvousChannel** | ~13% | ~4% | ~2.5% | **~16%** | Destabilized |
| **UnBufferedChannel** | ~22% | ~12% | ~7% | ~8% | Stabilizing |
| **SpinRendezvousChannel** | ~6% | ~6% | ~12% | **~19%** | Destabilized |
| **SynchronousQueue** | ~29% | ~4% | ~8% | **~21%** | Destabilized |
| **ArrayBlockingQueue** | ~7% | ~6% | ~7% | **~28%** | Catastrophic |

**Key finding:** 2P-4C **destabilizes** most implementations compared to any other configuration.

### Why Consumer-Heavy Creates Instability

**More consumers than producers means:**
1. **Consumer starvation** - most consumers waiting most of the time
2. **Unpredictable wake patterns** - which consumer gets next item varies
3. **Race condition amplification** - 4 consumers competing for 2 items
4. **Buffer drain chaos** (for ABQ) - rapid emptying creates edge cases

**SpinRendezvousChannel:**
- 4 spinning consumers + 2 producers = 2 consumers always wasting cycles
- Which 2 consumers connect with producers varies unpredictably
- Creates chaotic pairing patterns

**SynchronousQueue:**
- Dual-mode algorithm may thrash between stack/queue modes
- Consumer-side parking/unparking with high consumer count
- Producer scarcity causes frequent mode switches

**ArrayBlockingQueue:**
- Buffer repeatedly empties with 4 consumers draining faster than 2 producers fill
- Catastrophic Fork 2 result (21K) suggests deadlock-like condition
- Empty buffer forces all consumers to block, then thundering herd on wakeup

## The Producer Bottleneck Phenomenon

### Comparing Producer vs Consumer Bottlenecks

**When consumers bottleneck (4P-2C):**
- SpinRendezvousChannel: Only -6.7% degradation
- SynchronousQueue: -29.7% degradation
- ArrayBlockingQueue: +53.8% improvement
- Lock-based: Minimal impact

**When producers bottleneck (2P-4C):**
- SpinRendezvousChannel: -33.7% degradation (5× worse!)
- SynchronousQueue: -39.6% degradation (1.3× worse)
- ArrayBlockingQueue: +19.4% improvement (worse than 4P-2C)
- UnBufferedChannel: +30.1% improvement (surprising!)

**Why producer bottleneck hurts more:**

1. **Spinning efficiency:** Spinning producers generating work > spinning consumers waiting
2. **Production is serial:** Only 2 producer threads generate all work
3. **Consumption is parallel:** 4 consumer threads compete for scarce work
4. **Wakeup patterns:** Producer waking 1 of 4 consumers = more variance

## Design Insights from Consumer-Heavy Testing

### Lesson 1: Producer Bottleneck > Consumer Bottleneck

Across all implementations, **producer scarcity hurts more** than consumer scarcity:
- Spin-based: Consumers waste more cycles waiting than producers do
- SynchronousQueue: Coordination algorithms optimized for producer availability
- ABQ: Empty buffers create more problems than full buffers

### Lesson 2: Variance Patterns Reveal Coordination Complexity

**Stable under consumer-heavy:** UnBufferedChannel (8% CV)
- Simple signaling doesn't break down with consumer imbalance
- Already at performance floor, little room for variance

**Unstable under consumer-heavy:** Everyone else (16-28% CV)
- SpinRendezvousChannel: Chaotic consumer pairing (19% CV)
- SynchronousQueue: Algorithmic thrashing (21% CV)
- ArrayBlockingQueue: Buffer starvation pathology (28% CV)
- RendezvousChannel: Coordination timing breakdown (16% CV)

### Lesson 3: Buffering Asymmetry

ArrayBlockingQueue's contrasting behavior:
- 4P-2C: +53.8% (buffering helps with scarce consumers)
- 2P-4C: +19.4% (buffering helps less with scarce producers)

**Why:** Full buffers are stable; empty buffers create chaos.

### Lesson 4: Simple Algorithms Handle Edge Cases Better

UnBufferedChannel's surprising resilience:
- Only lock-based implementation to **improve** at 2P-4C (+30.1%)
- Simpler than RendezvousChannel (-20.4% at 2P-4C)
- Sometimes `signalAll()` "thundering herd" is more stable than targeted signaling
